My research asks how humans (and particulary babies) build internal models of structured environments. From babies discovering the regularities of their mother tongue, to adults accumulating evidence to take a decision or temporal structre in their perceptions. I approach these questions through three complementary lines.

## Methods

I rely on a broad methodological toolkit:

- **Behavioural experiments**
- **EEG, MEG, intracranial EEG**, including neonatal recordings (EEG)
- **MRI** (anatomical) and **fMRI** (task-based)
- **Computational modelling**: Mathematical account of behavior, Bayesian observers, Recurrent Neural Networks

## Background

I joined **CentraleSupélec** in 2015 for an engineering MSc with a major in *bio-engineering and signal processing*. I completed a parallel MSc in **computational biology** at *Paris-Saclay University*. Before my PhD, I have spent some time at **University College London** (with M. Chait, on auditory salience and pupillometry) and at the **McGill Montreal Neurological Institute** (with R. Zatorre, P. Albouy and B. Morillon, on speech and music processing).

I obtained my **PhD in cognitive neuroscience** at NeuroSpin (CEA & Sorbonne University) in 2023, supervised by Ghislaine Dehaene-Lambertz, on the learning of temporal dependencies in auditory sequences across adults and neonates.
